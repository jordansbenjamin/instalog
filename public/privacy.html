instalog: Privacy Policy

Last updated: 20 June 2026

instalog turns plain-text timesheet notes into Jira worklogs. To do that on your behalf it connects to your Atlassian (Jira) account using OAuth 2.0. This page explains exactly what data instalog accesses, why, and how it is stored.

What instalog accesses and stores

  • Your Atlassian profile: name, email, and account ID (via the read:me scope), used to identify your session and show your name in the app.
  • Your Jira site: the cloud ID and site URL of the Jira instance you grant access to, used to know where to post worklogs.
  • OAuth tokens: the access and refresh tokens Atlassian issues, used to post worklogs to Jira on your behalf. These are encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM) and are never sent to your browser.
  • A session cookie: an httpOnly, Secure cookie that keeps you signed in. It holds only an opaque session ID, never a token.

What instalog does not do

  • It does not sell, rent, or share your data with third parties.
  • It does not run advertising or third-party tracking.
  • It does not read your Jira data beyond what is needed to validate tickets and post worklogs.
  • It never exposes your Atlassian tokens to the browser or to anyone else.

Scopes requested

read:me (identity), read:jira-work (validate tickets), write:jira-work (post worklogs), and offline_access (refresh the access token so you don't have to reconnect hourly).

Storage and security

Data is stored in a MongoDB database. Tokens are encrypted before they are written and decrypted only server-side when posting a worklog. The session cookie is httpOnly and SameSite=Lax, so it is not readable by JavaScript and is not sent on cross-site requests.

Deleting your data

Clicking Disconnect in the app immediately deletes your stored tokens, profile record, and session. You can also revoke instalog's access at any time from your Atlassian connected apps settings.
